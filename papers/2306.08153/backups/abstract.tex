% Matrix factorization (MF) mechanisms for differential privacy (DP) have substantially improved the state-of-the-art in privacy/utility/computation tradeoffs for ML application over the ubiquitous DP-SGD algorithm in a variety of settings, including: 1) centralized training with $\epsilon > 2$ and a modest number of training steps (e.g., $\dimension \le 10^5$), and 2) cross-device federated learning where each device participates once in training. 

% In this work, we greatly expand the applicability of MF, showing that it can fully subsume DP-SGD. The key technique throughout is the construction of MF mechanisms with banded matrices. For cross-device federated learning, this enables multiple-participations with a relaxed device participation schema compatible with practical FL infrastructure. For centralized training, we give privacy amplification results for MF that are identical to the best known results for DP-SGD (corresponding to a diagonal matrix in MF), but can be strictly improved in most scenarios by amplifying a matrix with $\bands > 1$ bands.  Finally, for both domains using $\bands$-banded matrices substantially reduces the memory and time complexity of per-iteration noise generation from $\calO(\dimension)$, the total number of iterations, to $\calO(\bands)$ (a constant independent of $\dimension$).
